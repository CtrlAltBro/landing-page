## Three parts

| Part           | Responsibility                                                         |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Parent browser | Dashboard, rules, schedules, commands and PC status                    |
| Server         | Authentication, PostgreSQL storage, KV signals and API contract        |
| Windows PC     | Local enforcement by the service; sensor and UI in the child’s session |

The parent browser contacts the Worker over HTTPS. On the PC, the service contacts the API; the session app uses the local pipe `\\.\pipe\ctrlaltbro`. The agent opens no network port to receive commands.

## The service enforces rules

The service runs as SYSTEM through WinSW. It keeps rules and counters in `%ProgramData%\CtrlAltBro`, supervises the session app and enforces limits even when that app is closed.

The Electron app runs without elevation in the monitored session. It observes the foreground application, sends measurements and displays messages and time-up screens. If the sensor is unavailable, the service falls back to less precise counting based on running processes.

A standard account normally cannot stop the service or modify its protected files. An administrator retains control of the machine.

## From pairing to rules

1. `POST /api/agent/v1/pair` exchanges a one-time code for a device token.
2. `POST /api/agent/v1/ping` checks signals about every **30 seconds**.
3. `POST /api/agent/v1/sync` uploads pending data and receives rules and commands.
4. `POST /api/agent/v1/bye` reports shutdown or sleep.

A rule change or command updates `rev:` in KV. The next ping detects the change and triggers synchronization. When the parent views the PC, `view:` enables fast mode at **15 seconds**.

Pending command results, queued events, a screen-time batch or a forced upload also trigger a full sync. Outside fast mode, screen time is uploaded in batches about every 15 minutes.

A normal ping uses KV. A cold token cache requires a PostgreSQL lookup to rebuild it. Full synchronization uses PostgreSQL.

:::note[Rules do not arrive instantly]
The delay includes the next ping and Workers KV propagation. KV is eventually consistent: do not promise a strict maximum of 15 or 30 seconds. See [how KV works](https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/concepts/how-kv-works/).
:::

## Presence and alerts

KV holds `tok:` for tokens, `rev:` for changes, `view:` for a viewing parent and **`pres:` for presence**. Presence records are kept for a week and refreshed about every four minutes, or when health changes or a clean departure occurs.

- **Online**: contact less than six minutes old, without a recorded clean departure.
- **Offline**: reported shutdown/sleep, or presence that has become too old.
- **Silent**: no contact for at least 20 minutes without a clean departure, with a previously signed-in child, between 7 am and 11 pm in the PC’s time zone, and the last contact within 24 hours.

“PC online” and “child signed in” are different states. Silent alerts appear in the dashboard; email and push notifications are not currently provided.

## Trust limits

The pipe is accessible to local users. The client announces its SID; the service does not authenticate it by verifying the calling executable. Measurements are cross-checked against observed processes, but this is not full pipe authentication.

Monitored accounts are selected by SID. Rules and counters remain PC-wide: monitoring multiple children does not create separate quotas.
